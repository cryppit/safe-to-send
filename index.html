Explained

Everything, in plain words

Safe to Send answers one question before you send crypto: if I send to this address on this chain, can the money be reached? There's no sign-up and no wallet to connect.

The idea

Think of an address as a house number, and a chain as a city. The same number can exist in many cities. In one city it's your house. In another it's an empty plot, or somebody else's house.

Your wallet usually can't see this. It only checks that the address looks valid, so it lets you send. Safe to Send looks at what's really at that address on the chain you picked, and on the others, before the money moves.

How a check works

  1. 1. Pastethe address and the chain you are sending on
  2. 2. Recognisewhich network the address belongs to, and whether it has a typo
  3. 3. Readthe address on every EVM chain at once
  4. 4. Look upSafe owners, smart-account code and the Abstract wallet list
  5. 5. Answerone plain answer, with the reasons, before the money moves

The five answers

Every check ends in one of these.

  • Safe

    Someone can reach funds sent here: a normal wallet, or a Safe that exists on this chain.

    Check it's the right person, then send.

  • Caution

    It might work, but something needs checking: a contract, or a Safe with different owners on another chain.

    Ask the recipient to confirm first.

  • Stop

    Funds sent here are likely lost: a wallet from another chain, a token contract, the wrong network or a typo.

    Don't send. Get the right address.

  • Can't tell

    The address has never been used on any chain read, or a chain didn't answer.

    Send a small test first, or try again.

  • Format OK

    The address is the right format for that network. Bitcoin, Solana and most others aren't read at all.

    This is a typo check only. It isn't a safety answer.

What it catches

Key wallets work everywhere

A normal wallet address comes from a private key. The same key controls that address on every EVM chain, so funds sent on the "wrong" EVM chain can still be reached by importing the key there.

Smart wallets live on one chain

Safes and other smart wallets are contracts. They exist only where they were created. On any other chain the same address has no code and no key behind it. Some can be created there later; some never can.

Same address, different owners

A Safe can sit at one address on two chains with different people in charge. This compares the owners on every chain it reads, and warns you when they don't match. In 2022, that's how 20M OP ended up with someone else.

Abstract wallets can't follow you

An Abstract wallet can never be recreated on BNB Chain, Ethereum or Base, because Abstract computes addresses differently. ZKsync Era works the same way as Abstract, so there its owner can most likely recreate it. The archive keeps every Abstract wallet, so this still works after 15 Dec.

The wrong network stops here

It recognises 21 address families, tells you which one you pasted, and offers to check it where it belongs. Sui and Aptos addresses start with 0x too, and they don't fool it.

Tags and memos, where a ledger shows them

XRP and Stellar accounts can say they need a tag or memo. This reads that setting, and on XRP it also spots accounts that look shared. Most accounts declare nothing, so "no sign" never means "not needed".

Sanctioned and listed addresses

An exact match on the US OFAC sanctions list gets a Stop. A reserve wallet that an exchange published itself gets a dated note. An address on neither list gets no comment, because no list covers everything.

Names work too

Paste an ENS name such as vitalik.eth. It finds the address the name points to on Ethereum, then checks that address on the chain you picked.

Typos get caught, where they can be

Most addresses carry a built-in typo check, and this runs it. It also catches sending to a token's own contract, where tokens usually stay stuck.

What it can't tell you

  • Whether someone is honest. This is not a scam check. A scammer's wallet shows Safe, because the scammer can reach the money.
  • Who owns an address. It tells you funds can be reached, not by whom. Always confirm the address with the recipient.
  • Anything live on most non-EVM networks. Bitcoin, Solana and others are checked by format only, and nothing is read from them. XRP and Stellar are read only for tag and memo settings.
  • Whether you can skip a tag or memo. Most exchange accounts don't declare that they need one, so "no sign" isn't a yes. Ask the recipient.
  • Whether a tag is right. Only the recipient knows.
  • Every typo. Solana, Sui, Aptos, Movement, Starknet and Fogo addresses have no built-in typo check, so a mistyped one can still pass.
  • Whether an exchange will credit you. It doesn't recognise exchange deposit addresses, or know which networks and tokens an exchange accepts. More on exchanges
  • The future. An answer is true for the moment it was read. Contracts and owners can change.

How it's tested

Every answer comes from the same code, checked against a fixed set of real addresses — a known Abstract wallet, a normal wallet, token contracts, a mistyped one — one for each kind of answer, across every chain. That set runs on every change, and the public conformance board shows the current pass/fail. If a change ever made the tool answer one of these wrong, the board would show it.

Already sent it?

Paste the address and the chain you sent on. The same answer shows who could still reach the funds.

  • A normal wallet. Whoever holds the key can reach the funds on that chain, usually by adding the network or importing the key there.
  • A Safe on another chain. It may be possible to create the same Safe on this chain. That doesn't work for every Safe, so ask the owners quickly.
  • An Abstract wallet on another chain. Nobody can recover it, except on ZKsync Era, where the owner most likely can.
  • Sent to an exchange. See Exchanges below, and only use the exchange's official site.

Watch for recovery scams. People who lose funds get targeted. Never share your seed phrase, and ignore anyone who messages you offering to get the money back.

Exchanges

An exchange deposit address usually looks like a normal wallet, so this tool can say Safe even when the exchange won't credit you. The exchange's own records decide that, and this tool can't see them.

  • Wrong network or an unlisted token. The funds often arrive but never show in your account. Some exchanges will recover them for a fee, from a dollar or so to hundreds of dollars. Others refuse.
  • Missing memo or tag. The exchange has the funds but can't tell whose they are. You'll have to prove they're yours.
  • Below the minimum. Several exchanges won't credit or return these at all.
  • Withdrawn to the wrong exchange. Once it's on chain, the exchange you sent from generally can't cancel it. The receiving exchange holds the funds, so contact them with the transaction hash, and ask the sending exchange to back up your claim.
  • Sent to someone else's deposit address, or their memo. The money is usually credited to that person. Exchanges often exclude this from recovery, because the balance now belongs to someone else.

Before any large transfer: copy the address fresh from the deposit page, match the network on both sides, include the memo if there is one, and send a small test first.

Your privacy

  • No account, no tracking. There's no sign-up, analytics or cookies, and nothing is saved in your browser.
  • No Cryppit server. Your browser does the work. The page itself blocks every connection except the public services below.
  • A full check is visible to public services. Your browser asks each chain's public RPC service about the address, so those services can see the address and your IP address. An ENS name goes to Ethereum's. XRP and Stellar checks go to the XRP Ledger and Stellar public services.
  • A format check stays with you. On every other network it runs in your browser and sends the address nowhere.
  • The lists are checked in your browser. The sanctions and exchange lists are downloaded whole, so your address isn't sent to look them up.
  • Links can carry an address. A link with ?address= in it shows that address to anyone you share it with, and to the site host.
